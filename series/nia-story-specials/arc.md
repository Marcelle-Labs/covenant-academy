# Nia Story Specials — Arc Spec (`arc.md`)

**Form:** long-form story special, experimental
**Voices:** two — Abba (`qwynn-narrator`) and Nia (`nia-companion`)
**Runtime:** ~25–30 min per episode
**Status:** `pending_parent_review` — Pilot 01 drafted, nothing recorded, nothing cleared
**Authorization:** one pilot only. This spec authorizes no bulk production.

---

## 0. What this series is

Every other Academy series is single-voice: Abba narrates, and the cast appears
as described rather than voiced. This one is the experiment that asks whether a
second voice in the room earns its production cost.

The shape under test:

- Abba carries the factual and canonical story spine.
- Nia carries curiosity, noticing, retrieval, child-scale response, and
  source-humility.
- Nia solo passages speak directly to the listener, more intimate than her
  exchanges with Abba.
- Short Abba ↔ Nia dialogue scenes test whether the interaction sounds like two
  people rather than one script split in half.

The question the series exists to answer is narrow: **does the dialogue add
enough value to justify the complexity?** A negative answer is a real outcome.
If two voices do not beat one, the Academy stays single-voice and this series
ends at one episode.

This is also the first Academy material long enough to test Nia at length. The
`nia.md` brief and Character Canon v2 both gate bulk production behind exactly
one sourced pilot of roughly this size, and this is that pilot.

## 1. Pilot 01 — Miriam: Watch by the Water

`miriam-watch-by-the-water/script.md`

Miriam and the infant Moses at the Nile, from Exodus 1:15–2:10, KJV baseline.
Governed by *Miriam at the Nile — Authority Pack v1*.

Six beats, three Nia solo passages, and three dialogue labs. The labs are
deliberately isolated so each can be produced and judged on its own:

| Lab | Register under test |
|---|---|
| **A — Source Humility** | ordinary conversational turn-taking carrying source reasoning |
| **B — Playful Correction** | humour, comic timing, being wrong out loud and enjoying it |
| **C — Quiet Register** | slow, low, real space between turns, no theatrical sadness |

Three registers because a two-voice format that only works in one of them has
not been proven. Playful and quiet are the two most likely to fail, so both are
written in rather than left for a later episode.

The formation target is **notice before concluding** — the same target the
school-start track names, given a second body in the room. Nia models the
posture by being wrong, being corrected, and finding that interesting.

## 2. Deferred concepts

**Ruth** and **Esther** are named here as candidates and nothing more. Neither
has an authority pack, and neither may be drafted until one exists and is
accepted, exactly as Miriam was. No beat, no character handling, no framing, and
no runtime is implied by listing them.

Recording a candidate is not the same as approving it. If the pilot's answer is
that dialogue does not earn its cost, both candidates lapse rather than carrying
forward automatically.

## 3. Gates

1. **Authority pack** — accepted before any drafting. Miriam has one; Ruth and
   Esther do not.
2. **Script review** — parent review of the drafted script. Pilot 01 is
   `pending_parent_review` and has not passed.
3. **Take review** — cold listen of the recorded audio, separate from the script
   gate.
4. **Then**, and only then, a decision about whether there is a second episode.

Nia's `final_requirement` is `family_recorded` and the household roster contains
no one who can satisfy it for an eight-year-old girl. Any synthetic Nia is a
pacing prototype. None is ever child-facing cleared. That question is open in
`canon/nia.md` §9 and this series does not close it.

## 4. Open
